# Blind art review: world and HUD

Reviewed only the two supplied A/B comparison images at their displayed 430 px panel width. Target: cheerful Wii Sports simplicity with Switch Sports polish on a phone.

## Stronger world: A

A communicates a designed course more convincingly. The broad foreground, uneven tree line, distant fairway, and pale bunkers make the route and depth readable immediately. Light and surface variation connect the player to a substantial landscape. Its grass texture is busier than ideal for a phone, but the overall space feels finished.

B has the friendlier simplified palette and character silhouette. However, the repeated angular trees and nearly uniform green ground make the course feel like a corridor assembled from repeated pieces. Bright fairway bands suggest direction, but do less to establish actual terrain shape.

Both world panels are landscape captures, with different framing and visible HUD coverage. A's larger foreground and wider-feeling composition help it; this is a judgment of these captures, not proof of what either scene could achieve from another camera.

## Stronger HUD for a phone: A, provisionally

A has a clear distance hierarchy, readable throw/disc choices, and generous touch targets. The top and bottom grouping leaves the action recognizable. The bottom instruction panel is large for its small amount of text, but the intended interaction is immediately understandable.

B keeps more of its landscape playfield unobstructed and supplies a useful course overview. At the supplied width, though, its map details and small readouts are much harder to read. The visible video-player header also reduces the usable game view.

This comparison is unequal: A is a full portrait capture, while B is a shallow landscape video capture scaled to the same width. A has substantially more pixels and vertical room. The phone-readability preference is defensible; a definitive HUD-quality ranking needs equivalent aspect ratios, game states, and capture framing.

## Biggest remaining gap and concrete fix

The simplified world's terrain needs more visibly shaped land. In world B, the bright central strip largely sits inside a continuous green corridor; it needs distinct foreground, middle-distance, and destination forms.

Create one broad, gently rising fairway with an asymmetric shoulder and a visible low swale beside it. Widen the nearby clearing, break the tree wall into irregular clusters, and expose one pale soil or sand edge near the destination. Use broad soft lighting to describe those slopes. Keep the simple materials; the improvement should come from readable land shapes rather than added grass texture.
