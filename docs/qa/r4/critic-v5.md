## Canopy closure

- **Close portrait: NO.** The foreground broadleaf trees read as separate, continuous shaded crowns; the corrected v4 attribution stands. Their outline now looks angular and cut into coarse sections, rather than softly irregular with small leafy protrusions. B and C show this most clearly along the foreground crown's top and right edge.
- **Wide tee and flyover: NO.** The smaller crowns are less revealing, but F's left-side broadleaf row still reads as ridged solid forms. This view does not override the close-view failure.

## Exactly one largest remaining gap

**Canopy: coarse polygonal edge breakup still dominates the crown silhouette.** More irregularity is visible, but its angular scale makes the crowns look chiseled rather than leafy. This is a contour problem, not evidence of several balls within one tree.

The actionable fix is to preserve the continuous rounded main volume while replacing the conspicuous angular cuts with numerous shallow, uneven leaf-cluster protrusions and notches. At this 430-pixel presentation, the foreground edge needs disturbances roughly a couple of screen pixels deep, rather than large corners that reshape whole sections of the crown. The visible acceptance condition is a continuous shaded crown with a fine, irregular leafy edge in the portrait capture, still coherent in the flyover.
