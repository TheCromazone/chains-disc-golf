## Original canopy closure: NO

The close portrait view is decisive. In B and C, the nearest broadleaf crown still resolves into a few inflated forms: its conspicuously round right shoulder reads as a large ball attached to the rest of the crown. The shared shading helps the mass hold together, but does not hide those component shapes. A and D read more readily as single crowns with irregular leafy edges.

F's smaller, more distant crowns make this less conspicuous, but the left-side broadleaf row still presents coarse rounded humps. The wide flyover therefore does not supply evidence that the close-view problem is resolved.

## Exactly one biggest remaining gap

**Canopy: the crown envelope still exposes large rounded component lobes.** This is the remaining version of the original spherical-assembly problem. The required visible fix is to integrate the prominent circular shoulder into the primary crown and break its outer contour with smaller, uneven foliage projections. Broad shading must continue across that unified volume so the result reads as one thick crown rather than several attached balls.

This closure judgment comes from the crowns in both framings, not from how much screen area the ground occupies.

## Attribution correction after isolated-crown diagnostics

Inspected `diagnostic-0.png`, `diagnostic-1.png`, `v4-high-tee.png`, and `v4-low-tee.png` at original resolution.

**I withdraw the claim that the round right shoulder is an attached component of one crown.** The diagnostic images visibly separate it into another complete tree. My earlier proposed fix to merge that shoulder into the neighboring crown was therefore wrong.

The isolated crown in `diagnostic-1.png` reads as one continuous mass with a darker underside. It does **not** visibly read as several attached balls. The visible part of `diagnostic-0.png` also supplies no sound evidence of that assembly defect, although its left side is cropped. Returning to the full captures, the apparent two-ball shape is consistent with overlapping trees. The original spherical-assembly allegation is closed by this visual evidence.

**Overall canopy closure: NO, for one narrower remaining gap: insufficient foliage-scale breakup of the crown edge.** The isolated full crown still has a largely smooth, coarse polygonal contour, particularly across the top and around the right side. It reads as a solid rounded object more readily than as a leafy canopy. The full portrait captures retain that appearance; the wide flyover makes it harder to see rather than contradicting it.

The required visible fix is modest, uneven leaf-cluster projections and small notches along the contour, including the lower edge, while preserving the continuous shaded mass already present. Do not add more large lobes or merge neighboring trees. This finding supersedes the earlier attached-ball attribution and its proposed remedy; the pair winners remain A, D, and E.
