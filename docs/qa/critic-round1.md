# Blind visual review — round 1

Reviewed only `blind-world.png` and `blind-clubhouse.png`. Judgments concern the supplied still images and the target of family-friendly, premium console sports presentation.

## World pair — choose A

A has a more convincing playable space: the fairway, tree line, terrain shading, and distant scenery establish depth and guide the eye toward the shot. B has a friendly silhouette and readable tee position, but its broad green surfaces look unfinished beside A.

**Single biggest gap in B:** insufficient environmental depth. Similar bright greens and weak distance separation flatten the course into a foreground plane and a wall of trees.

**Concrete correction:** give the course three distinct depth bands using richer foreground contact shadows, a clearly shaped mid-distance fairway, and progressively lighter, less saturated distant trees. Preserve the simple geometry and verify that the throwing route reads without the dotted guide.

## Clubhouse pair — choose B

B more convincingly delivers premium sports character presentation: the expressive face, recognizable hairstyle, clothing detail, and confident pose make the avatar feel authored. Its restrained interface gives that character a clear stage. A does have the stronger immediately readable main action at the supplied size.

**Single biggest gap in A:** the hero avatar lacks personality in its presentation. The featureless back view and stiff stance make the central figure read as a placeholder, despite occupying much of the screen.

**Concrete correction:** pose the clubhouse avatar in a relaxed three-quarter front view, with a visible expressive face and an asymmetrical disc-holding stance. Frame the head and torso against a quiet background so the character becomes the recognizable host of the screen.

## Comparison limits

These are preferences from unequal compositions, not controlled wins. Each pair mixes portrait and landscape captures; their displayed widths are also different. Portrait images devote substantially more screen area to their scenes. This affects character size, interface readability, and perceived empty space. The clubhouse images show different functions—a round launcher and an avatar selector—so their action density cannot be compared directly. Still images cannot establish animation quality, interaction clarity, responsiveness, or actual device legibility.
