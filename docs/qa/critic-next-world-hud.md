# Blind world and HUD critique

Judged only the two supplied A/B images. Target: Wii Sports simplicity with Switch Sports polish. These are still-image judgments; interaction quality is untested.

## World: B is stronger

B reads as a playable place: rolling ground, changing fairway widths, foreground-to-background scale, varied vegetation, and a clear opening toward the target. Its saturated grass and conspicuous texture are dated, but the terrain makes the shot feel spatially interesting. A reads as a flat corridor bounded by repeated cone trees. Its clean colors are friendly; the repeated silhouettes, straight trunk rhythm, and nearly level ground make the environment feel assembled from placeholders.

**Single biggest world gap:** terrain composition. A has a destination marker but little landform shaping the journey toward it.

**Actionable revision:** give A one broad, visible rise or dip across the middle distance, bend the fairway around an asymmetric tree group, and break the opposing tree rows with a clearing. Use three restrained ground values to describe those forms. Do that before adding small props or more surface detail.

**Comparison limit:** both panels have similar width, but B is taller and A devotes much more of its already shorter frame to interface. B therefore has more opportunity to show its world. Even allowing for that disadvantage, the visible terrain in A is substantially less developed.

## HUD: A has the stronger game-first composition

A keeps the course and shot setup dominant and concentrates useful context in the corner map. The screenshot's YouTube bar is capture chrome, not a game HUD defect. Some in-game text is too small to judge at this size, so this is a composition win, not proof of better phone readability.

B has clearer large text, consistent rounded panels, and understandable throw/disc selectors. However, it looks like a mobile app surrounding a game viewport. The wide status banner, four stacked utility buttons, persistent power gauge, selectors, and large instruction sheet all ask for attention at once. Meanwhile, a large blank sky region separates the status information from the shot. The target is obvious; the actual basket and landing area are much less readable.

**Single biggest HUD gap:** B's allocation of screen space and attention. The framing and permanent interface reduce the visual importance of the shot.

**Actionable revision:** reframe B so the horizon sits nearer the upper third and the landing area occupies more of the central screen. Collapse the permanent instruction sheet to a compact contextual swipe hint, group utility controls behind one menu, and show the power gauge when a throw is being prepared. Keep the readable throw/disc selectors and a compact distance/wind readout. Verify the result at its actual portrait phone size.

**Comparison limit:** A is a short landscape capture and B is a full portrait screen at approximately the same displayed width. They have radically different total pixel areas and framing constraints. A cannot establish portrait touch usability; B cannot claim a cleaner hierarchy simply because its labels are larger. A wins visible restraint, while B wins visible label legibility.

## Priority

The largest overall obstacle to the requested feel is the scene's flat, repetitive composition. Simplifying the HUD will expose that weakness more clearly, so improve the landform and camera framing together. More glass panels, icons, or decorative props will not close this gap.
