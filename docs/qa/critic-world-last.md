# Blind world critique

**Stronger world: B.** It more convincingly delivers the bright, welcoming sports-resort setting. The rolling fairway, sunlit grass, tree shadows, varied tree silhouettes, and distant haze create a continuous playable landscape with clear depth. It feels closer to a finished console sports game.

A is readable and cheerful, but its broad, nearly uniform green surfaces and repeated angular trees make the environment feel like an early low-poly prototype. The lighter fairway stripes help direction, yet do little to suggest terrain or lighting.

**Single biggest remaining gap: convincing ground depth and lighting in A.** The ground occupies most of the world view, so its flat shading dominates the impression. Gentle land contours with coherent sunlit and shaded areas would make the course feel like a physical place. This matters more than adding extra props or interface polish.

**Comparison limit:** Both views are 430 pixels wide, but B is taller and has much less HUD coverage. A therefore gets substantially less unobstructed landscape space, which disadvantages its sense of scale and immersion. Even allowing for that difference, B's visible terrain and lighting are stronger.
